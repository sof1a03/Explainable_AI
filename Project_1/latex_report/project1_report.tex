\begin{abstract}
This study applies explainable AI techniques to detect pleural effusion in chest X-rays using a DenseNet169 model.
Saliency Maps highlight image regions relevant to predictions, while Example-Based Explanations retrieve similar cases for comparison. Explanation quality is assessed using Quantus for Saliency Maps and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG) for case rankings. The explanations presented in this work show good initial results but would still require improvement before being applied in the field.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Machine learning is increasingly used to solve complex problems across domains.
In healthcare AI supports diagnostic and treatment decisions~\cite{e2024evaluating}.
However, many models act as black boxes with limited insight into how they generate outputs~\cite{ahmed2022explainable}, making explainable AI essential to improve transparency and trust.
X-ray analysis is one of the main applications of AI in medicine, especially for detecting bone and lung diseases~\cite{miro2022evaluating}.
This paper presents a machine learning model to predict pleural effusion from CXR images.

\section{Methods \& Materials}\label{sec:methods-materials}
\subsection*{Dataset and Preprocessing}

The dataset used for this study was a smaller version of the CheXpert dataset~\cite{ashery_chexpert}, containing frontal and lateral chest X-rays, of which only the frontal images were used.
Among the pathology labels available, particular attention was paid to \emph{Pleural Effusion}, which served as the classification target.

Images with missing or uncertain \emph{Pleural Effusion} labels were removed to improve reliability and all images were resized to \(224 \times 224\) pixels and normalized to the \([0, 1]\) range.
To address the class imbalance, class weights were calculated based on label frequencies and applied during training, where an $80$-$20$ train test split was used.
Since the original X-rays were grayscale, each image was duplicated across three channels to match the input format required by ImageNet-pretrained models.

\subsection*{Model and Training}

A \textbf{DenseNet169} \cite{huang2017densely} convolutional neural network was used for the \emph{Pleural Effusion} classification. The model was initialized with ImageNet \cite{deng2009imagenet} weights, freezing the lower layers while fine-tuning upper layers. A global average pooling layer was added, followed by dense layers with ReLU activations. The final layer used a sigmoid activation for binary classification. Training was performed using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $0.0001$, binary cross-entropy loss, and class weighting. The model was trained for $10$ epochs and saved.

\subsection*{Explanations}

For this experiment two different explanations were exploited. This report explores Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (\textbf{Grad-CAM}), which identifies critical regions in an image by leveraging gradients in the last convolutional layer of a CNN and global-average-pooling to assess neuron importance \cite{selvaraju2017grad}. Grad-CAM generates heatmaps that highlight relevant features for model predictions. To evaluate the quality of saliency maps, the Quantus toolkit \cite{hedstrom2023quantus} was used, employing metrics such as Pixel-Flipping \cite{bach2015pixel} (Faithfulness), Local Lipschitz Estimate \cite{alvarez2018towards} (Robustness), Model Parameter Randomisation \cite{adebayo2018sanity} (Randomisation), Relevance Mass Accuracy \cite{Arras_2022} (Localization), and Complexity \cite{bhatt2020evaluating} (Complexity).

For example-based explanations, image feature embeddings from the last pooling layer are extracted and ranked using \textbf{cosine similarity}, measuring vector orientation and magnitude \cite{han2022similarity}. These rankings are compared to expert-defined rankings via Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (\textbf{nDCG}) \cite{jarvelin2002cumulated}, which assesses ranking quality by assigning relevance scores in the range $[5.5,1]$, computing discounted gain, and normalizing against the ideal expert ranking. This ensures that the model groups similar images in a clinically meaningful way.

\section{Results \& Discussion}\label{sec:results-discussion}
The following sections present and discuss the results of testing the model and explanation methods from Section~\ref{sec:methods-materials} on predefined test images.
\subsection*{Model Training and Evaluation}

\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
\begin{minipage}{0.4\textwidth}
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{Trainin_validation_acc_loss.png}
    \caption{\small Training and validation per epoch}
    \label{fig:performance}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}{0.54\textwidth}
During training, the DenseNet169 model steadily improved in training accuracy and loss, though validation accuracy fluctuated, indicating some overfitting (Figure~\ref{fig:performance}). The final model achieved 79.21\% accuracy on the test set, with a precision of 63.41\% and recall of 81.25\%, showing good detection of positive \emph{Pleural Effusion} cases.
The F1-score was 71.23\%, balancing precision and recall, while the ROC AUC reached 84.96\%, demonstrating strong overall discriminatory power. %A confusion matrix was computed (Table \ref{tab:confusion})
A binomial test yielded a p-value of $2.03 \times 10^{-17}$, confirming the performance is significantly better than chance.
\end{minipage}
\end{figure}

\subsection*{Saliency Maps}\label{subsec:res:saliency-maps}
Figure \ref{fig:gradcam} shows the GradCAM explanations generated to gain insights about the models' focus on the test images.
The most important region, as identified by the model, is represented in seismic colors: red, white, and blue.
The bounding boxes on the explanation boxes circle the critical parts of the image, that the explanation should focus on.
\begin{figure}[h!]
\label{fig:gradcam}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{grad_cam}
\caption{\small GradCAM Explanations}

In all five test images, the explanations highlight parts of the bounding box but also often highlight irrelevant areas outside of it.

In most cases, the heatmaps focus on the lower lung regions associated with Pleural Effusion, suggesting the model identifies clinically relevant areas.
However, some explanations highlight unrelated regions, indicating that the model occasionally relies on uninformative features to classify Pleural Effusion.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
In the medical field, understanding the decision process of machine learning models is essential for transparency and trust in the findings. Thus, increasing the importance of integrating explanations in assistance systems provided to medical workers. In this study, two explanation methods were presented for this purpose and assessed for their quality. Overall, the results showed promising outcomes, especially in terms of having a close ranking compared to a professional.
However, improvements upon the model and explanation could be made with additional time and different classification models should be compared in the future, preferably using the PyTorch environment for better integration with Quantus.
